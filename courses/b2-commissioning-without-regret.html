Commissioning without regret: buy the method, not the number

Most survey regret is bought at the commissioning stage, weeks before anyone sees a result. A vendor quotes a number of completes and a delivery date; what you actually need to know is who could be reached, how they were picked, how the response rate will be counted and what the weights will be. Tonight you write the brief for Ottervale, a constructed town council choosing between a bus route and longer library hours, so the survey it buys can carry that decision.

Part 1 · covers mode and vendor

Pick the mode by who it can reach

5 min live

A mode is not a channel preference; it decides who had any chance of being asked. A postal survey to addresses drawn at random reaches people who live at an address on the list. An online opt-in panel reaches people who signed up to one. A street intercept reaches whoever walks past. Each is the right tool for some question, and the cheaper and faster modes buy their speed by giving up control over who could be reached. Start from the decision, not from the quote.

LiveFour modes, what each can reach4 min▶

Postal to a random address sample. Draws addresses at random from a household list and mails a questionnaire, usually with reminder waves. Reaches most people in private households. Misses people in communal establishments such as care homes and halls, and anyone who never opens the post. Slow: the reminder waves are where the time goes.

Online opt-in panel. People who signed up to answer surveys, usually for small rewards. Fast, and usually the cheaper quote. Nobody was picked at random from the town, so the panel reaches only the kind of person who joins panels, and a printed margin of error has no accepted basis. Part 3 is the argument about whether models can repair that.

Phone. Calls numbers drawn from a list or generated at random. Response has fallen hard: Pew reports its typical phone survey's response rate went from 36% in 1997 to 9% in 2012. Still a probability method; the question is who picks up.

Street intercept. An interviewer stops passers-by. Fast and vivid, useful for quick feedback on something people just used. It reaches whoever walks that street at that hour, and nobody else, so it should never carry a number for the whole town.

Cost against error, in words: every step toward speed and lower cost in this list is also a step away from knowing who could have been reached. Analyst session 3, Who you could have reached, measures each frame's coverage on the lab.

Self-studyQuestions to ask a vendor before the quote3 min▶

1 · What list will you draw from, and who is not on it? A vendor that cannot name its frame cannot tell you who is missing.

2 · Is the sample a probability sample? If not, what model will connect it to the town, and on which variables?

3 · Which response rate will you report? Name the AAPOR Standard Definitions rate, and send the counts behind it.

4 · How many completes per area and age band do you expect? The total is the headline; the smallest cell is what limits you.

5 · What will you weight to, and to which totals? Named variables and named sources, not "representative".

6 · Will you report the design effect and the honest margin? A weighted survey's margin is wider than the simple-random one.

7 · Are you willing to publish the AAPOR Transparency Initiative disclosure items? Part 2 lists them. A vendor that hesitates here is telling you something.

Real world

Disclosure as a buying standard. The American Association for Public Opinion Research runs a Transparency Initiative: member organisations commit to disclose a fixed list of methods items for every survey they release, from sponsor and wording to sample generation, weighting and limitations. A buyer can make the same list a condition of the contract, which turns a vendor's methods into something you can check rather than trust. (AAPOR, Transparency Initiative, read at source.)

Part 2 · covers the methods statement

The methods statement you demand before fieldwork

5 min live

The cheapest moment to get a methods statement is before you sign. After delivery, every missing item becomes an argument; before fieldwork, it is a line in the contract. The AAPOR Transparency Initiative gives you a ready list of 12 items, and AAPOR's Standard Definitions give you the vocabulary for the one number vendors most often report loosely, the response rate.

LiveThe 12 items, and what to ask for Ottervale4 min▶
Disclosure itemWhat Ottervale asks the vendor to write down
1 · Data collection strategySurvey, panel wave or something else, and how the parts fit
2 · Sponsor and conductorThe council as sponsor; the vendor and any subcontracted fieldwork
3 · Exact wordingEvery question as asked, in order, including the bus and library items
4 · PopulationAdults 16 and over living in Ottervale, and what "living in" excludes
5 · Sample generation and recruitmentThe frame, whether the sample is probability or not, any quotas, any incentives
6 · Modes and languagesPost, web, phone or a mix; which languages were offered
7 · DatesFirst and last day of fieldwork, and when reminders went out
8 · Completes and precisionNumber of completes; a margin for a probability sample, a precision statement for a non-probability one
9 · WeightingVariables, target totals and their source, or a statement that none was used
10 · Processing and quality checksHow speeders, duplicates and incomplete forms were handled
11 · LimitationsWho the design could not reach, in the vendor's own words
12 · Use of AIWhether AI drafted questions, coded open answers or did anything else

The list is AAPOR's Transparency Initiative disclosure set, read at source; the right-hand column is the constructed Ottervale version.

Self-studyWhich response rate, by name2 min▶

AAPOR's Standard Definitions, now in their 10th edition (2023), define a family of response, cooperation, refusal and contact rates, the response rates running from RR1 to RR6. They differ mainly in how they treat partial interviews and cases whose eligibility was never settled, so one fieldwork can honestly produce several rates. Write into the brief which rate the vendor reports, and ask for the case counts by final outcome so anyone can recompute it. The formulas live in the Standard Definitions themselves; read them there rather than from a summary.

Nonresponse in customer satisfaction surveys after a support contact, where the answerers are the customers who chose to reply, is a different problem with its own fixes, taught in AI Customer Support, QA you can trust ↗. This page covers commissioning a survey of a whole population.

Real world

The disclosure list grew with the field. AAPOR's current Transparency Initiative list now includes the use of AI alongside the long-standing items on wording, sampling and weighting, and it asks non-probability surveys for a precision statement rather than a margin of error. Both changes are worth copying into a contract word for word, because they close the two gaps buyers most often leave open. (AAPOR, Transparency Initiative disclosure elements, read at source.)

Part 3 · covers opt-in panels against probability samples

Opt-in panels: an argument still running

5 min live

The opt-in panel quote will usually be the fastest and cheapest on your desk, so you need a position on it. The profession does not have one settled answer. One side says avoid opt-in panels when you need a population number; the other says accuracy sits on a continuum and the right model can carry a non-random sample a long way. Both sides cite real evidence. Tonight you hear both, see what the lab shows, and decide for Ottervale without pretending the argument is over.

LiveSide A: avoid them when you need the population value2 min▶

AAPOR's 2010 report on online panels advised researchers to avoid nonprobability online panels when the aim is to estimate population values accurately. It found no accepted theoretical basis for projecting them to a population, and the studies it reviewed generally found them less accurate than probability methods against benchmarks. The report itself called its conclusions tentative.

Pew's 2016 test then ran nine nonprobability samples from eight vendors against benchmarks. Across them, the average estimated bias was 15.1 points for Hispanic respondents and 11.3 for Black respondents. The same study has a twist Side A must carry honestly: Pew's own probability panel had the lowest bias on non-civic items (4.1) but tied for the largest on civic engagement (13.4). In 2024 Pew found that among opt-in respondents under 30, 12% claimed a licence to operate a nuclear submarine, and 20% agreed the Holocaust is a myth against 3% in a probability survey.

LiveSide B: a continuum, and the model does the work2 min▶

AAPOR's 2013 task force on non-probability sampling reached a different emphasis. There is no single framework that covers all non-probability methods; they sit on a continuum of expected accuracy; inference rests on modelling assumptions that must be stated; and the most promising methods are model-based. The price is transparency: a non-probability survey carries a higher burden to show its working.

The best-known demonstration is Wang, Rothschild, Goel and Gelman (2015). They polled Xbox users in the 45 days before the 2012 US presidential election, 750,148 interviews from 345,858 people, a sample far from representative on age and sex. After multilevel regression and poststratification (MRP), which models the answer within many small cells and reweights the cells to the population, their estimates were in line with leading poll aggregators.

Self-studyWhy raking the lab's panel did not fix it3 min▶

On the lab, the opt-in panel with no weights is biased by -5.9 points over 1,000 repeats and its margin covers the truth 40% of the time. Raking it to the census on age, sex and area leaves -5.7, covering 49%. The address list, stratified and raked the same way (R3), sits at -0.4 and covers 94%.

The reason is built into the simulation, and it is exactly the case weighting cannot reach. The panel's reach was simulated to depend on age and, within every age group, to be lower for people in fair, bad or very bad health, the very thing being measured. Raking can make the panel's age, sex and area totals match the town, but inside each of those groups it still has too few people in worse health, and no weight on age, sex or area can see that. Weights move people between groups; they cannot repair who is missing inside a group.

That is a statement about this simulated panel, not a verdict on panels. Side B's answer is a panel with auxiliary variables that do track the outcome, or a model such as MRP built on them. Whether such variables exist for Ottervale's question is the thing to ask the vendor. MRP mechanics are not taught here.

Real world

Same data type, opposite stories. Pew's 2024 opt-in results for young adults and the Xbox study both used non-random online samples. One produced claims no probability survey would, the other matched the aggregators after heavy modelling. The difference was not the mode but what each did between collection and the published number. (Pew Research Center, "Online opt-in polls can produce misleading results, especially for young people and Hispanic adults", 2024; Wang et al., International Journal of Forecasting 31(3), 2015; both read at source.)

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and Leader session 2 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · When is the cheapest moment to get a vendor's full methods statement, and what should it cover?

Before fieldwork it is a contract line; after delivery it is a dispute. The AAPOR Transparency Initiative list gives the 12 items, and the response rate should be named by its Standard Definitions code.

2 · Pew's 2016 and 2024 opt-in studies and the 2015 Xbox study point in different directions. What is the fair reading?

The disagreement is live. AAPOR 2010 and Pew show large errors; AAPOR 2013 and the Xbox study show a continuum where model-based methods can work, with a higher transparency burden.

3 · On the lab, raking the opt-in panel on age, sex and area moved its bias only from -5.9 to -5.7 points. Why?

Weights move people between groups; they cannot add the people missing inside a group. The address list raked the same way (R3) reached -0.4, because its frame was not missing them.
