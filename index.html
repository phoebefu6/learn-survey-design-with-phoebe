Learn Survey Design & Sampling with Phoebe

A survey prints one number and one margin of error, and the margin describes only one of the ways the number can be wrong. The others arrive earlier: the question's wording, the list you drew from, who you picked, who answered, and how the answers were weighted. These ten sessions follow one question all the way through: how far is this survey's number from the truth, and which choice put it there? The bench answers it for real. It asks a question of 20,000 adults from the Census 2021 teaching sample of England and Wales, where the true answer is known, and measures every choice against it. Six Analyst sessions are for the people who design, field and weight surveys. Four Leader sessions are for the people who commission them, read them and decide with them.

Analyst track 📋 · six sessions, hands-on in the browser

For analysts, researchers and data scientists who write the questionnaire, design the sample, weight the responses and report the number with an honest margin. Name every error, rewrite a leading questionnaire, choose a frame and a size, spend the budget on a design, weight the people who stopped answering back in, and score the whole plan in the survey error lab.

🎯Analyst session 1 · start here

Every way a survey misses

Total survey error: specification, coverage, sampling, nonresponse, measurement, processing and adjustment. Why the printed margin covers one of them, and a first run of the lab.

 ✏️Analyst session 2

Questions that do not lead

Wording, double-barrelled and leading items, balanced scales, the midpoint and don't know, order and context effects, cognitive pretests. Rewrite Ottervale's draft.

 📮Analyst session 3

Who you could have reached

Target population against frame: address lists, online panels, street intercepts, the households a frame never lists, and sample size for a target margin with a response rate.

 🧮Analyst session 4

Designs that spend the budget well

Simple random, stratified, clustered and systematic samples; design effect and effective sample size; why clustering widens the margin and stratifying can narrow it.

 ⚖️Analyst session 5

Who stopped answering, and weighting them back

Response rates against nonresponse bias, taught as the live disagreement it is; post-stratification and raking to census margins; trimming; what weights cost in variance.

 🧪Analyst session 6 · bench night

The survey error lab

The full ladder on 20,000 real census adults: from a street intercept to raking and trimming, the nonresponse no weight can see, and Ottervale's plan scored with an honest margin.

Leader track 🧭 · four sessions, no code

For managers, policy and insight leads who commission surveys and act on them. Read any survey in five questions, buy one without regret, decide what its number can carry, and report it with the full margin and the methods that let a reader check it.

🔎Leader session 1 · start here

Read a survey in five questions

Who was the population, who could be reached, how were they picked, who answered, how was it weighted: the methods box most reports bury, read first.

 🤝Leader session 2

Commissioning without regret

Choosing mode and vendor, what to demand in the methods statement, and opt-in panels against probability samples, taught as the live disagreement it is.

 🚌Leader session 3

What the number can carry

The decision the survey informs, the error it can tolerate, subgroup cells too small to report, and when a survey is the wrong tool. Ottervale's bus against library call.

 📰Leader session 4 · capstone

Reporting it honestly

The full margin with its design effect, the methods statement, the transparency items, what not to headline, and why a survey describes but does not explain.
